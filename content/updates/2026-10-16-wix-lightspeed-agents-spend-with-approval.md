On October 15, two small-business platforms announced AI agents that can handle spending, with the owner approving first.

Wix said its Symphony agents can now pay on an owner's behalf through Stripe's Link wallet. According to Wix, the owner sees "the merchant, purchase and exact checkout amount" and approves in Link, which then issues a single-use credential. The agent doesn't receive the underlying card details. Wix's blog says the current version "asks for a yes on every single purchase." Approving a budget once is listed as a future direction, not a feature today. The feature is rolling out to Symphony users with a US-based Link account, in US dollars.

Lightspeed said Lightspeed AI can now identify low-stock products, prepare purchase orders, update product data and plan inventory transfers. Per its release, it "shows a preview of what action it will take" and "waits for the retailer's explicit approval," and actions are recorded in an audit log. It's an opt-in beta for select customers, with broader availability "coming soon."

Neither announcement states what these features cost.

## What it means for an SMB

- **An approval step is the right default.** If a tool can spend or order on your behalf, look for a preview and an audit trail.
- **Set your own rule first.** Decide which purchases you'd approve from a phone and which need a second person. Our [AI approval matrix](/guides/ai-approval-matrix) has a "spend or commit money" row you can adapt.
- **Treat each "yes" as a real check.** Look at the merchant and the amount before you approve.
- **Ask vendors for the log.** You should be able to see what the agent proposed and what you approved.
